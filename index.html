Scientific Name:

Physarum vestigium

Habitat:

Damp sediment floors beneath old colony growths, where spore density is uneven enough to be worth searching.

Discovered by / at:

Shruthi Swaminathan, NYU Shanghai

Appearance:

A soft lumpy body with no front and no head, ringed by seven tapering arms that each end in a five petal cap. Its colour is never fixed. The whole organism drifts through the spectrum together, darker at the base of each arm than at the tip.

Family:

Thought to descend from the slime molds, which solve mazes without a brain by wandering, leaving a trail, and following the trail they left.

Behaviour:

It drifts constantly, whether or not anyone is watching. It leans toward attention, closing a small fraction of the distance each moment rather than arriving. Left alone for long enough it stops following, slows its arms and folds its caps shut.

Feeding:

It draws the ground in toward itself. Spores within reach shrink away while it feeds, and its energy rises. That energy leaks away constantly, so a creature that is ignored visibly shrinks and pulls its arms in.

Trace:

It does not erase where it has been. Each moment only partly covers the last, so a ghost of its path stays behind it on the ground, fading rather than vanishing.
